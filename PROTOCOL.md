# Evaluation protocol (fill in before collecting data)

Use one copy per tool. Commit it to your project before the first case is collected, so the plan cannot drift to fit the results.

## 1. The tool
- Name, version and date accessed:
- What it claims to do, in one sentence:
- Who would use it, and at what point in care:

## 2. The reference standard
- What counts as the truth for each case (for example: stereomicroscopy after extraction, laboratory test, expert panel):
- Who measures it, and are they blind to the tool's output?
- Known weaknesses of the reference standard:

## 3. The cases
- Where cases come from, and the inclusion and exclusion rules:
- Planned number of cases, and why that number is enough:
- Do these cases look like the people the tool will meet in practice (age, language, devices, settings)?

## 4. The comparator
- Who does this task today (for example: nurse, clinician, current software)?
- Will they judge the same cases, under the same conditions?

## 5. What will be measured
- Primary outcome (for example: accuracy against the reference standard), with a 95% Wilson interval:
- Secondary outcomes (sensitivity, specificity, agreement, calibration, time per case):
- Paired comparison with the comparator (exact McNemar test):

## 5a. If clinicians will use the tool (dyad design)
- Will each case be read by the clinician alone first, then with the AI output in view (or by separate clinicians)?
- Pre-specify the reliance outcomes: accepted wrong AI advice, harmful switches, beneficial switches, overrides of a correct AI.
- Will some AI outputs be deliberately wrong (planted errors) to measure automation bias? How will participants be debriefed?

## 5b. After deployment
- Minimum acceptable accuracy, fixed now: ____ ; minimum cases per review period: ____ ; review period: ____
- Who acts when a period or site is flagged "re-qualify", and what happens to the tool in the meantime?

## 6. Failure review
- Every error will be listed and reviewed. For each: what went wrong, would a clinician have caught it, could it harm a patient?

## 7. Privacy and safety
- What data leave the institution, if any? (Default: none.)
- Approval needed (IRB, data-use agreement):
- How the tool's output is kept from affecting real patient care during the study:

## 8. Reporting
- Results will be reported whatever they show, including where the tool fails.
